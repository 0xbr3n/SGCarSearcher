How to read the numbers

Listed dep vs true dep

A dealer's depreciation figure divides the price by whatever period flatters the car. This app recomputes it as (price − what you get back at the end) ÷ years you actually get. On a PARF car the two are usually close. On a renewed-COE car the listed figure is often much kinder than reality, because there's no ARF rebate waiting at the end.

PARF schedule changed in Feb 2026

Cars first registered before 13 Feb 2026 keep the old schedule: 75% of ARF back if deregistered under 5 years old, stepping down 5 points a year to 50% at 9–10 years, nothing after 10. Cars registered from 13 Feb 2026 get a far thinner 30%-down-to-5% schedule. Rebate caps: none before 15 Feb 2023, $60,000 from then, $30,000 from Feb 2026. Every used car you're looking at now is on the old schedule.

The 10-year cliff

At 10 years the ARF rebate goes to zero and you either scrap or pay PQP to renew — which in 2026 has been running near new-COE money. A car with 2 years left is cheap because you're buying 2 years and a scrap ticket. That's fine if you've decided that's what you want; it's a trap if you assumed you'd keep it.

Mileage sanity

Roughly 15,000–17,000 km a year is normal in Singapore. Well under that on an older car can mean long idle spells rather than gentle use; well over usually means private-hire history. The app flags both.

Rebate figures here are estimates from LTA's published schedule. Before you commit money, check the actual car on OneMotoring's PARF/COE rebate enquiry — that's the number that binds.
